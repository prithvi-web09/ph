🪐 Gravitational Orbit Simulation

This simulation shows how planets move in stable or unstable orbits due to the Sun’s gravitational pull.

It helps us understand how changing the Sun’s mass directly affects the distance and shape of planetary orbits.

We can observe that a stronger gravitational force pulls planets closer and makes their orbits tighter.

When the Sun’s mass decreases, planets move farther away, showing weaker gravitational attraction.

he simulation demonstrates that inner planets are more sensitive to changes in the Sun’s mass.

It visually explains how gravitational force controls orbital speed and path of each planet.

We can see how extreme conditions can cause planets to spiral inward and get destroyed.

🧲 Magnetic Field around a Current-Carrying wire

5 A

This simulation helps students understand how a staright current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field.

It visually demonstrates that magnetic field lines form concentric circles around the wire.

The simulation helps explain the Right-Hand Thumb Rule in an interactive way.

This simulation shows that magnetic field strength decreases with increasing distance from the wire.

Students can verify that magnetic field strength is inversely proportional to distance from the conductor.

The effect of increasing current on magnetic field strength is shown visually and quantitatively.

Students can observe how magnetic fields behave symmetrically around a straight conductor.

It helps students connect theoretical concepts with practical visualization.

🔦 Double Slit Interference

550 nm 0.5 mm 0°

This simulation visually demonstrates the principle of superposition in wave interference.

It helps students understand how constructive and destructive interference patterns form on the screen.

By adjusting wavelength, slit separation, and phase difference, students can see how these factors affect the interference pattern.

The simulation allows students to explore the relationship between wavelength and fringe spacing.

Students can observe how increasing slit separation leads to more closely spaced fringes.

The effect of phase difference on the intensity and position of interference fringes is shown interactively.

This simulation helps students connect theoretical concepts of wave interference with visual patterns they can manipulate.
