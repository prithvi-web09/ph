🪐 Gravitational Orbit Simulation

This simulation demonstrates the concept of gravitational attraction between objects.

It helps visualize how mass influences gravitational force.

Larger masses produce stronger gravitational pull.

Objects closer together experience stronger gravitational force.

The simulation shows how planets move in orbital paths.

Orbital motion happens due to the balance between velocity and gravity.

If velocity is too low, the object falls toward the massive body.

If velocity is too high, the object escapes the orbit.

The simulation helps understand circular and elliptical orbits.

It visually represents Newton’s law of gravitation.

The user can change the mass of objects to observe different gravitational effects.

Increasing mass increases gravitational influence.

Decreasing distance increases gravitational attraction.

Objects move along curved paths due to gravitational pull.

🧲 Magnetic Field around a Current-Carrying wire

5 A

This simulation demonstrates magnetic field formation around a current-carrying wire.

It visualizes magnetic field lines that are normally invisible.

Magnetic field lines form circular loops around the wire.

The direction of the field depends on the direction of current.

The simulation demonstrates the Right-Hand Thumb Rule.

According to this rule, the thumb represents current direction.

The curled fingers show the direction of magnetic field lines.

The simulation shows how magnetic fields surround electric currents.

It helps visualize electromagnetic interaction.

Field lines are denser near the wire.

This indicates stronger magnetic field strength.

The field becomes weaker as distance increases.

The simulation demonstrates magnetic field distribution.

It explains the relationship between electricity and magnetism.

The magnetic field exists in the space around the conductor.

The simulation helps understand electromagnetic theory.

Magnetic field lines never intersect each other.

They form closed circular paths.

The direction of current affects the field orientation.

Reversing current reverses the magnetic field direction.

The simulation explains basic principles of electromagnetism.

It demonstrates how electric current produces magnetic effects.

The simulation visually represents Ampere’s law concepts.

It helps students understand field visualization.

The simulation connects theory with visual representation.

Magnetic fields are fundamental to many electrical devices.

This concept is used in motors and generators.

The simulation explains why magnetic compasses react to currents.

Field lines indicate both direction and strength of the field.

The simulation helps interpret magnetic field diagrams.

This simulation demonstrates the phenomenon of wave interference.

It shows how waves combine when they overlap.

The simulation visualizes constructive interference.

Constructive interference occurs when waves reinforce each other.

It also demonstrates destructive interference.

Destructive interference occurs when waves cancel each other.

The simulation explains how interference patterns form.

Bright regions represent constructive interference.

Dark regions represent destructive interference.

The simulation represents waves as continuous oscillations.

It shows how waves travel from the source.

When two waves meet, their amplitudes combine.

The simulation helps visualize the superposition principle.

It demonstrates how two wave sources create interference patterns.

The interference pattern depends on wavelength.

The distance between sources affects the pattern spacing.

The simulation explains the concept of phase difference.

Waves in phase produce constructive interference.

Waves out of phase produce destructive interference.

The pattern repeats periodically across space.

The simulation helps understand wave behavior in physics.

It explains how light behaves like a wave.

The interference pattern is similar to the double slit experiment.

This experiment historically proved the wave nature of light.

The simulation shows how waves propagate outward from sources.
